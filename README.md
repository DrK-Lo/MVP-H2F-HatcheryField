# MVP-H2F-HatcheryField

This repository contains data and code for a hatchery-to-field (H2F) experiment evaluating survival, growth, and genetic predictors of performance in Eastern oysters (*Crassostrea virginica*), part of the project "CAREER: Evaluation of machine learning algorithms for understanding and predicting adaptation to multivariate environments with a Model Validation Program (MVP)" (NSF award #2043905, PI: Dr. Katie Lotterhos).

## Authors
Kiran Bajaj, Nicole Mongillo, Madeline Eppley, Camille Rumberger, Zea Segnitz, Dr. Katie Lotterhos (Northeastern University) \
Shelley Katsuki, Dr. Ryan Carnegie, Dr. Jessica Small (Virginia Institute of Marine Science)

## Overview

Adult Eastern oysters were collected from 8 locations along the U.S. East Coast and brought to the Virginia Institute of Marine Science's Aquaculture Genetics and Breeding Technology Center (ABC). In May 2023, adults were strip spawned and offspring were reared at the ABC, then deployed at two common garden field sites in the Chesapeake Bay: the York River and Lewisetta, VA. Mortality and phenotype data were collected at six-month intervals over two years (2023–2025).

## Research questions

1. What are the differences in survival and growth among oysters from different parental groups through time in the hatchery and at different field sites?
2. To what extent do parental group, parental heterozygosity, and parental environment-of-origin predict hatchery success and long-term field performance?
3. Do pairwise differences in parental genetics and environment-of-origin predict pairwise differences in growth or survival?
4. What genomic regions are putatively under spatially heterogeneous selection?
5. How do different strategies for increasing diversity (e.g., crossing gametes from different genetic groups vs. combining seed from different groups) contribute to long-term success at different field sites?
